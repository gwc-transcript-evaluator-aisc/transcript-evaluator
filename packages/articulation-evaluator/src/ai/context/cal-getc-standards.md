# Cal-GETC Standards, Policies and Procedures

**Source:** [ICAS Cal-GETC Standards, Version 1.4](https://icas-ca.org/cal-getc-standards)
**Version:** 1.4 (an updated, corrected version of 1.3)

The 2026 Cal-GETC Standards, Policies and Procedures Version 1.4 is an updated, corrected version of the 1.3 version, created in response to the requirement that ICAS establish a singular lower-division general education pathway meeting the academic requirements necessary for transfer admission to the California State University and the University of California, per AB 928 (Berman, 2021). The document is intended to be both self-contained and accessible. It includes current practices and policies and, in some cases, generates new policies and procedures to be implemented by the California Community Colleges, the California State University, and the University of California. The Cal-GETC standards, policies, and procedures in this document are independent of any prior General Education requirements.

## 1. History

### 1.1 Purpose

The California General Education Transfer Curriculum (Cal-GETC) is the singular general education pathway for California Community College (CCC) students to fulfill lower-division general education requirements necessary for admission and transfer to both the California State University (CSU) and the University of California (UC). The curriculum and its policies are overseen by the Intersegmental Committee of the Academic Senates (ICAS), representing faculty from California's three segments of public higher education.

### 1.2 Background

Since the 1960 Master Plan for Higher Education, ease of transfer has been the cornerstone of California's three-tiered system of higher education. Beginning Fall 1981, CCC students could use the statewide CSU General Education-Breadth pattern (CSU GE) to meet lower-division general education requirements when transferring to the CSU.

In response to concerns raised by the Commission to Review the Master Plan and the Legislature, embodied in Assembly Bill 1725 (Chapter 973, Statutes of 1988), faculty from the CCC, CSU, and UC developed IGETC (Intersegmental General Education Transfer Curriculum) to provide a statewide, lower-division general education transfer curriculum applicable to all CCC students transferring to a CSU or UC campus.

IGETC differed for the UC and the CSU: the CSU required an additional Oral Communication course beyond the common IGETC pattern, while the UC required proficiency in a language other than English (LOTE). To reduce confusion from these differing pathways (CSU GE, IGETC for CSU, IGETC for UC, UC-specific patterns), AB 928 (Berman, 2021) required development of a singular lower-division general education pathway meeting academic eligibility and preparation for transfer admission to both the CSU and UC.

The Academic Senates of the CCC, CSU, and UC endorsed the creation of Cal-GETC to facilitate ease of transfer for CCC students regardless of destination campus. ICAS developed the Cal-GETC framework based on a modification of the IGETC pattern in Spring 2022 and approved a preliminary structure in Spring 2023. Use of the Cal-GETC transfer pathway began Fall 2025. Under Cal-GETC, every student is designated simply as having achieved, or not having achieved, Cal-GETC certification irrespective of transfer destination. Transfer students with catalog rights prior to Fall 2025 may maintain use of their CSU GE Breadth or IGETC pattern to the extent permitted by relevant programs and institutions.

## 2. Areas of Distribution for Cal-GETC

The California General Education Transfer Curriculum is comprised of courses taught at California Community Colleges that satisfy specific areas of general education:

- **Area 1 — English Communication** (three courses: one English Composition, one Critical Thinking and Composition, and one Oral Communication; 9 semester or 12 quarter units)
  - **1A: English Composition** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
  - **1B: Critical Thinking and Composition** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
  - **1C: Oral Communication** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
- **Area 2 — Mathematical Concepts and Quantitative Reasoning** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
- **Area 3 — Arts and Humanities** (two courses: one Arts and one Humanities; 6 semester or 8 quarter units)
  - **3A: Arts** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
  - **3B: Humanities** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
- **Area 4 — Social and Behavioral Sciences** (two courses from two academic disciplines; 6 semester or 8 quarter units)
- **Area 5 — Physical and Biological Sciences** (two courses: one Physical Science and one Biological Science; one of the two must be associated with a one-semester/one-quarter-unit laboratory (Section 9.5.3); 7 semester or 9 quarter units)
  - **5A: Physical Science** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
  - **5B: Biological Science** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
  - **5C: Laboratory** — 1 semester or 1 quarter unit
- **Area 6 — Ethnic Studies** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units). Must be in Ethnic Studies or a similar field, provided the course is cross-listed with Ethnic Studies.

**Summary — Total:** 11 courses (34 semester or 45 quarter units). All courses are 3 semester or 4 quarter units, except Area 5C, which is 1 unit.

## 3. Students Who May Use Cal-GETC

Completion of Cal-GETC permits a student to transfer from a CCC to a CSU campus/program without needing additional lower-division GE coursework after transfer to satisfy campus GE requirements. Completion of Cal-GETC generally permits transfer to a UC campus/program without needing additional lower-division GE coursework after transfer, though some individual UC colleges or majors may not accept Cal-GETC for all GE requirements (see the UC Admissions "general education" web page for that list). It is strongly recommended that students complete Cal-GETC prior to transfer for flexibility in class selection and timely progress to degree.

The CSU will honor completion of lower-division GE coursework at a CCC provided the course was on the approved GE certification list in assist.org when completed. CSU transfer students who are Cal-GETC certified still need to complete 9 semester units of upper-division GE after transfer and may be held to other campus-specific graduation requirements.

### 3.1 Cal-GETC and Other Lower-Division General Education Options

Cal-GETC completion is neither an admission requirement nor an admission guarantee, nor the only way to fulfill lower-division GE requirements. It may be required for some programs (e.g., an ADT). Under AB 928 it is the "singular lower division general education pathway that meets the academic requirements necessary for transfer admission to the California State University and the University of California."

UC students may alternatively complete a seven-course pattern to fulfill UC's systemwide Eligibility Areas plus major preparation. CSU students may elect to fulfill CSU GE after transfer, but this would be inconsistent with the intent of AB 928.

Cal-GETC may not be appropriate for some engineering, math, or science students, or majors with heavy lower-division unit loads, especially without a Transfer Model Curriculum/ADT. Such students may focus on major preparation while meeting minimum admission requirements (e.g., the UC seven-course pattern) and would not be Cal-GETC certified prior to transfer. CLEP cannot be used for Cal-GETC (Section 6.3), though the CSU has a separate systemwide policy for CLEP credit toward CSU GE.

### 3.2 Students Who Are Eligible to Use Cal-GETC

ICAS developed Cal-GETC for CCC transfer students. A student may be Cal-GETC certified if they have completed coursework at any CCC, regardless of current enrollment status or units accrued.

### 3.3 Restrictions for Returning Students

Students who initially enroll at a UC campus, leave to attend a CCC, and return to the same UC campus ("readmits") cannot use Cal-GETC. The CSU has no systemwide policy prohibiting Cal-GETC use for students returning to the CSU from a CCC.

## 4. Cal-GETC Course Database and Certification

Once approved, a course appears on the Cal-GETC course list on ASSIST (assist.org), giving counselors and students electronic access to articulated CCC courses. Certification is honored provided the course was on the college's approved Cal-GETC certification list on ASSIST when completed. Courses listed in more than one area can only be applied to one area per student during certification.

## 5. Cal-GETC Course Eligibility (Courses That Can Be Used for Cal-GETC)

### 5.1 Cal-GETC Course Submission and Review Process

The UC and CSU conduct an annual joint review of CCC courses submitted for Cal-GETC. Decisions are announced each Spring and updated on ASSIST.

#### 5.1.1 Effective Date

An approved course carries Cal-GETC certification effective the fall term of the academic year after submission (if active in the curriculum at approval time, or will be active the next fall term). *Example: a course submitted December 2026 and approved May 2027 becomes effective Fall 2027.* Denied courses receive detailed reasons and may be revised and resubmitted the following cycle.

#### 5.1.2 Re-Evaluation of Existing Approvals

Courses found to no longer meet standards during periodic re-review are phased out but remain on the approved list for at least two academic years, giving the CCC time to submit a revised course outline of record. *Example: notified Spring 2026 that a course no longer meets a standard; it remains effective through Summer 2028.*

#### 5.1.3 Textbooks

- Textbooks must be identified in the Course Outline of Record (COR) and published within seven years of submission, or clearly identified as a classic.
- Open Educational Resources (OER) or online texts are acceptable if constant and publicly available as published textbooks (not merely a list of web links); the COR must include title, author, and publication date.
- Laboratory science courses must have a clearly identified Laboratory Manual in the COR (publisher-provided or faculty-compiled).

#### 5.1.4 Cross-Listed Courses

Cross-listing curriculum must be approved (e.g., by the college curriculum committee or board) before COR submission. Cross-listed CORs must be identical in title, description, objectives, content, methods of instruction, methods of evaluation, textbooks/readings, and assignments (prefix/number may differ), and must appear together (or with annotation) in official college publications.

### 5.2 Cal-GETC Courses and Cal-GETC Certification

#### 5.2.1 Intra-Segmental Transfer of Cal-GETC Courses Towards Cal-GETC Certification

- *Example 1:* If College A certifies using coursework from College B, College A must place the course according to College B's approved Cal-GETC certification list, regardless of how College A's similar course is classified.
- *Example 2:* A course approved only in Quantitative Reasoning at College A but only in Social and Behavioral Sciences at College B cannot be used by College A to meet the Quantitative Reasoning requirement via College B's version.

A course lacking Cal-GETC area approval cannot be applied to certification (exception: IGETC, see Section 10.2).

#### 5.2.2 Applying CSU and UC Courses to Cal-GETC

A UC or CSU course counted for a GE area that corresponds to and could qualify for a Cal-GETC area is generally appropriate to certify toward that area. A course without a GE designation cannot be applied.

#### 5.2.3 Applying Independent, Private, and/or Out-of-State Higher Education Courses to Cal-GETC

Appropriate non-CCC GE courses in the humanities, mathematics, social sciences, and natural sciences completed at U.S. institutions accredited by ICAS-approved accreditors should be routinely included (e.g., Psychology, Sociology, Economics, Political Science, Biology, Chemistry). Care should be taken reviewing course outlines for content, prerequisites, texts, units, and Cal-GETC Area Standards (Section 9), especially for:

- **Area 1B** (Critical Thinking and Composition) — few non-CCC colleges combine both in one course.
- **Area 1C** (Oral Communication) — note differences from CSU GE Area A1.
- **Area 6** (Ethnic Studies) — narrow eligibility constraints unlikely to be met by a course not specifically targeted.

A CCC may include such non-CCC lower-division courses if:

1. CCC faculty in the discipline (or designee, e.g., Articulation Officer) deem it comparable to a course on that college's approved Cal-GETC list; or
2. If no comparable course exists at the certifying CCC, but a comparable course exists at another CCC on its Cal-GETC pattern, and the CORs are comparable in content, prerequisites, texts, units, and conformity to Area Standards; or
3. If no comparable course exists at any CCC, the certifying CCC may use the non-CCC course if it conforms to the Area Standards; or
4. If the non-CCC course was completed prior to the CCC course's Cal-GETC effective date and meets criterion 2, it may be applied; or
5. If UC has determined the course meets minimum transfer admissions eligibility via the UC seven-course pattern (e.g., UC-E, UC-M, UC-S), it may be applied.

**ICAS Approved Accreditors:**

- Accrediting Commission for Community and Junior Colleges (ACCJC), Western Association of Schools and Colleges
- Higher Learning Commission (HLC)
- Middle States Commission on Higher Education (MSCHE)
- New England Commission of Higher Education (NECHE)
- Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities (NWCCU)
- Southern Association of Colleges and Schools Commission on Colleges (SACSCOC)
- WASC Senior College and University Commission (WSCUC)

#### 5.2.4 Upper Division Courses

Non-CCC courses applied to Cal-GETC should generally be lower-division. Exceptions:

- A UC or CSU campus has classified a course/series as upper-division but requested systemwide lower-division transfer credit (e.g., some Economics, Organic Chemistry, Adult Psychopathology/Abnormal Psychology offerings).
- A non-CCC course determined comparable to a CCC course approved for Cal-GETC may apply regardless of upper-division status, if it meets area standards and criteria.
- A CSU uses an upper-division course to fulfill a "lower-division" CSU GE requirement in an area where CSU GE and Cal-GETC standards don't differ (e.g., Area 3A Arts, 3B Humanities, or Area 4 Social and Behavioral Sciences).

#### 5.2.5 International Coursework

International coursework may apply only if the international institution has U.S. accreditation from ICAS-approved accreditors. Otherwise it cannot be applied; students with substantial non-U.S.-accredited international coursework should be encouraged toward CSU GE or campus-specific UC GE patterns instead.

#### 5.2.6 Coursework Taught in a Language Other Than English

Some U.S. coursework taught in a language other than English at an ICAS-accredited institution may be used, but CORs must be submitted for review in English. **Exception:** Area 1 courses (1A, 1B, 1C) must be taught and delivered in English. There is no limit on the number of courses from other ICAS-accredited U.S. institutions that can be included.

#### 5.2.7 Distance and Correspondence Education

**5.2.7.A CCC Courses:** CCCs may use distance and correspondence education for Cal-GETC if approved by the CSU and UC during the Cal-GETC course review process; delivery modality does not determine approval. Distance education is defined under CCC Title 5, Chapter 6, Subchapter 3, Section 55200 (instruction where instructor and student are separated by time/distance, interacting via technology). Correspondence education (Section 55260) is defined as education via mailed/electronically transmitted instructional materials and exams to students separated from the instructor, with limited, student-initiated, non-regular interaction; typically self-paced with a regular assignment/feedback cycle. A course part correspondence and part residential is considered correspondence.

**5.2.7.B Non-CCC Courses:** Distance and correspondence education from non-CCC institutions may be used with the same scrutiny as other non-CCC courses (Section 5.2).

### 5.3 Courses Appropriate for Cal-GETC

Courses must be both CSU and UC transferable. There is no limit on the number of courses from ICAS-accredited institutions that can be included.

### 5.4 Courses Not Appropriate for Cal-GETC

#### 5.4.1 Courses That Focus on Personal, Practical, or Applied Aspects

Cal-GETC content must be presented theoretically, focusing on core concepts and research methods of the discipline. Examples that do **not** qualify: Everyday Legal Problems, Beginning Drawing, News Writing, Physical Education, College Success, Library Science, Child Development: Implications for Child Guidance.

#### 5.4.2 Introductory Courses to Professional Programs

Courses such as Introduction to Business, Set Design for Theater, and Writing for Commercial Markets lack sufficient breadth for GE and are excluded.

#### 5.4.3 Independent Study or Topics Courses

Independent study and special topics courses are not acceptable, since content-varying-by-term makes Cal-GETC applicability indeterminate.

#### 5.4.4 Summary of Non-Applicable Courses

Courses not transferable to CSU/UC via Cal-GETC include, but are not limited to:

- Pre-baccalaureate courses (including remedial English composition)
- Variable Topics
- Directed Study
- Independent Study
- Personal, Practical, Skills Courses
- Introductory courses to professional programs
- Performance Courses
- Creative Writing
- Logic
- Computer Science
- Trigonometry, unless combined with college algebra or pre-calculus
- Course outlines of record not written in, or translated to, English
- International coursework from institutions lacking ICAS-approved accreditation

#### 5.4.5 Minimum Units and Unit Restrictions on Courses for Cal-GETC Certification

Courses with fewer than 3 semester or 4 quarter units cannot carry Cal-GETC certification, and three 1-unit courses cannot substitute for a 3-unit requirement (a sequence of 1-unit courses generally lacks the depth/scope/rigor of a single 3-unit course).

**Exception:** 3-quarter-unit or 2-semester-unit Math and English courses satisfying Area 1A or Area 2 may be applied if part of a sequence, where at least two of the sequence courses are each completed with a "C" or higher (2.0/4.0), and the sequence meets Cal-GETC rigor and breadth.

- *Example 1:* A stand-alone 4-semester-unit Area 4 course satisfies Area 4.
- *Example 2:* A stand-alone 2-semester-unit Area 4 course does **not** satisfy Area 4.

## 6. Credit by External Exams

There is no limit on the number of external exams applicable to Cal-GETC, regardless of when taken. Cal-GETC certification does not determine applicability toward the receiving institution's program requirements.

### 6.1 Advanced Placement (AP)

A score of 3, 4, or 5 is required. Students with AP credit should not also take a comparable college course (transfer credit won't be granted for both).

- No AP exam is equivalent to Area 1B (Critical Thinking and Composition).
- Where more than one area is possible (e.g., 3B or 4), the AP exam may be used in either, regardless of where the certifying CCC's comparable course is located.
- A score of 3–5 on a Physical or Biological Science AP exam earns credit toward Area 5A or 5B **and** satisfies the Area 5C (Laboratory) requirement. With this exception, each AP exam applies to only one area.
- An acceptable AP score generally equates to 3 semester or 4 quarter units, except:
  - Biology, Chemistry, Physics 1, or Physics 2: 4 semester or 5 quarter units toward Area 5.
  - Environmental Science, Physics C: Mechanics, Physics C: Electricity/Magnetism: 3 semester or 4 quarter units.

### 6.3 College Level Examination Program (CLEP)

CLEP cannot be used for Cal-GETC.

### 6.4 Other Exams

Other College Board and ACT exams (e.g., SAT I, SAT II, Subject/Achievement Tests) cannot satisfy Cal-GETC requirements. Credit by exam is acceptable only with a transcript from a U.S. college/university (ICAS-accredited) specifying course title, unit value, grade, and posted term. A "Credit/Pass" designation is acceptable if institutional policy equates it to a "C" (2.0/4.0) or higher, and the course is deemed comparable by CCC faculty or designee (Section 5.1).

## 7. Unit Value

A course must have a minimum of 3 semester or 4 quarter units, except laboratory courses accompanying lecture courses (Section 9.5.3). Three 1-semester-unit courses cannot substitute for a 3-unit requirement, as a sequence of 1-unit courses generally lacks the depth, scope, and rigor of a single 3-unit course.

**Exception:** 3-quarter-unit or 2-semester-unit Math/English courses satisfying Area 1A or Area 2 may apply if: (1) part of a sequence, (2) at least two sequence courses each completed with "C" or higher (2.0/4.0), and (3) the sequence meets Cal-GETC rigor and breadth (Sections 9.1.1 and/or 9.2).

- *Example 1:* English 101/102/103 (3 quarter units each) — any "C"-or-better combination totaling 6 quarter units may satisfy Area 1A if it meets Section 9.1.1 rigor/breadth.
- *Example 2:* Math 121 (Calculus A) + Math 122 (Calculus B), 3 quarter units each, each completed with "C" or higher, equivalent to a single 6-quarter-unit Calculus course — both may apply to Area 2 if they meet Section 9.2 rigor/breadth.
- *Example 3:* English 100 + 105 (2 semester units each, each requiring ≥3,000 words) — both may satisfy Area 1A for a total of 4 semester units if rigor/breadth is met.

### 7.1 Combining Quarter and Semester Units

When combining unit values within an Area, convert all to one system (quarter or semester), to the student's benefit. *Example: Area 4 needs 6 semester or 8 quarter units. One 4-quarter-unit course + one 3-semester-unit course: convert 3 semester units to 4.5 quarter units (3 × 1.5), for a total of 8.5 quarter units — the ≥8 quarter-unit requirement is satisfied.* Note that such conversions may result in a student needing additional coursework to meet CSU's 120-semester-unit graduation minimum. *Example: two 4-quarter-unit courses = 8 quarter units = 5.33 semester units (< 6); a "fully prescribed" semester-unit major program might then require ~0.67 additional semester units.*

## 8. Grades

### 8.1 Minimum Grade Requirements

A minimum "C" grade (2.0/4.0) is required in each course. A "C-" (< 2.0) cannot be used for Cal-GETC certification.

### 8.2 Credit/No Credit — Pass/No Pass

"Credit/Pass" grades may be used if the college's policy states this is equivalent to a "C" (2.0/4.0) or higher. CSU/UC campuses may limit the number of Pass/No Pass units accepted toward transfer, graduation, and major requirements (e.g., UC allows a maximum of 14 semester units of Pass/No Pass toward the 60 transferable semester units required for transfer admission). No more than 14 semester units (21 quarter units) of Pass/No Pass, excluding credit by examination, may be used toward Cal-GETC certification.

## 9. Subject Areas and Course Guidelines

All Cal-GETC courses must be baccalaureate-level and transferable among all California public postsecondary segments. Courses listed in more than one area can only be applied to one area (laboratory exception, Section 9.5.3).

Cal-GETC courses shall be culturally broad, helping students understand human culture and social structures via a comparative, historically-grounded approach, and recognizing contributions from men, women, and various ethnic/cultural groups. Courses shall address modes of inquiry characterizing different areas of human thought: the nature of questions addressed, how questions are formulated, how analysis is conducted, and the validity/implications of answers obtained.

Coursework taught in a language other than English at an ICAS-accredited institution may be used, but CORs must be submitted in English. **Exception:** Area 1 courses must be delivered in English.

### 9.1 Subject Area 1: English Communication

(3 courses: 9 semester or 12 quarter units)

- **Area 1A: English Composition** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
- **Area 1B: Critical Thinking and Composition** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)
- **Area 1C: Oral Communication** — one course (3 semester or 4 quarter units)

#### 9.1.1 Subject Area 1A: English Composition

Focuses on the practices of academic writing: recursive writing stages, informed decisions across writing situations, and rhetorical awareness that transfers across the curriculum.

**9.1.1.A Course Content:**

- **Appropriate Readings** — must include a writing handbook or evidence of similar pedagogy, plus substantial reading.
- **Processes and Practices of Writing** — varied, flexible strategies for generating, drafting, revising in multiple genres/audiences, with formative peer and instructor feedback on major assignments.
- **Rhetorical Approach to Writing Instruction** — explicit strategies for navigating audience, purpose, context, genre, language conventions, and sources-as-evidence.
- **Types of Writing** — synthesis-driven argumentative texts, literature reviews, analytical essays; genre-transformation activities encouraged; creative writing genres excluded from main assignments.
- **Quantity of Writing** — minimum 5,000 words of scaffolded academic writing across major assignments.

Courses that do **not** fulfill this requirement include: Literature courses, Humanities content-focused courses, Creative Writing courses, English Language for Multilingual Students (ELMS) courses that are exclusively language-acquisition focused, and major-specific writing courses (e.g., Writing for Engineers, Journalism, Business Writing/Communication).

**9.1.1.B Additional Course Structures:**

- "Stretch"/"intensive" English Composition courses (blended transferable + developmental content) may be approved if all courses in the sequence comply with Section 7 and the transferable content is comparable to Section 9.1.1.
- English Composition for multilingual students may be approved if content is not predominantly developmental and is comparable to Section 9.1.1.
- Interdisciplinary sequences can be used (see Section 9.4).

#### 9.1.2 Subject Area 1B: Critical Thinking and Composition

Requires successful completion of Area 1A as prerequisite. Emphasizes development/refinement of critical thinking skills for evaluating and producing academic and argumentative writing, building on Area 1A's rhetorical approaches via critical reasoning, inquiry-driven research, and argumentative writing.

Courses shall emphasize formal/rhetorical components of argumentative writing necessary to:

- analyze, criticize, and generate complex ideas
- reason inductively and deductively
- identify assumptions underlying particular conclusions
- reflect critically on one's own thought processes
- respond appropriately to texts, attending to intended audience, purpose, and social context
- distinguish knowledge from belief and fact from judgment
- recognize common logical errors or fallacies of language and thought
- evaluate sources for relevance, reliability, and appropriateness to the rhetorical context

Requires a minimum of 5,000 words of writing (may include process drafts, peer response, and other informal writing), with revision/feedback on at least one extended argument. Courses should require significant reading/analysis and use a writing handbook or similar pedagogy, reflect cultural diversity in chosen texts, and attend to fairness, equity, and access in curricular design and assessment.

**9.1.2.A Restriction on Unit Distribution:** A single course fulfills Area 1B.

Courses commonly lack the required critical-thinking component if their description/objectives don't specifically include inductive/deductive reasoning, the relationship of language to logic, and analyzing/criticizing/advocating ideas — this must go beyond mere critical reasoning or literary criticism. For non-CCC coursework, CCC faculty in the discipline (or designee) determines comparability; since combined Critical Thinking + Composition courses outside the CCC system are uncommon, such certification is rare. Valid documentation (COR or syllabus) should be kept on file.

#### 9.1.3 Subject Area 1C: Oral Communication

Must be taught in English (Section 5.1.8). Provides foundational knowledge and practice in public speaking for informative and persuasive communication in a democratic society, and critical evaluation of others' speeches.

Students shall be exposed to coursework conveying and providing practice in:

- theoretical foundations of creating/sharing knowledge, including canons of rhetoric and Aristotelian proofs (ethos, pathos, logos)
- finding, examining, and using supporting materials from primary/secondary sources for credibility, accuracy, and relevance
- conceptualizing/using compelling arguments with an organizational pattern appropriate to audience, occasion, and context
- ethical communication practices (truthfulness, accuracy, honesty, reason)
- rhetorical sensitivity to diversity, equity, inclusion, belonging, and accessibility
- well-prepared, faculty-supervised/evaluated speeches delivered to a live audience with effective delivery techniques
- effective verbal/nonverbal delivery and managing communication apprehension
- critical listening and constructive peer feedback
- applying rhetorical principles to analyze historical and contemporary public discourse

The course must substantially focus on rhetorical principles; discipline- or career-specific oral communication courses are not a good fit.

**9.1.4 Restriction on Unit Distribution:** A single course fulfills Area 1C.

### 9.2 Subject Area 2: Mathematical Concepts and Quantitative Reasoning

(1 course: 3 semester or 4 quarter units)

Met by a transfer-level course in mathematics, statistics, or other quantitative disciplines, focused primarily on mathematics/quantitative reasoning and addressing students' ability to develop, present, use, and critique quantitative arguments. *Example: a statistics course must emphasize the mathematical basis of statistics, including probability theory, estimation, applications, and interpretation.*

Symbolic Logic and Computer Programming courses are generally unacceptable. A course on mathematical concepts for elementary school teachers will not be accepted if it primarily focuses on elementary-school competencies or teaching pedagogy rather than more advanced mathematical reasoning. A sequence of courses may be approved only if students must pass all courses in the sequence and the transferable content equals an approved Area 2 course.

**9.2.1 Restriction on Unit Distribution:** A single course fulfills Area 2.

### 9.3 Subject Area 3: Arts and Humanities

(2 courses: 6 semester or 8 quarter units) — at least one Area 3A (Arts) and one Area 3B (Humanities) course.

Fulfilled by two courses encouraging analysis/appreciation of works of philosophical, historical, literary, aesthetic, religious, and cultural importance. Students shall be exposed to coursework that:

- develops historical understanding of major Western and non-Western civilizations/cultures via philosophy, language, literature, religion, and fine arts
- recognizes contributions from men, women, and various ethnic/cultural groups
- encourages analysis/appreciation of works of philosophical, historical, literary, religious, and cultural importance
- constitutes the humanizing perspective at the heart of liberal arts general education

*Note: CSU campuses may allow Area 3A and/or 3B courses to also satisfy the CSU U.S. History, Constitution and American Ideals (AI) graduation requirement. Not every class needs to individually meet every element above — e.g., a class might focus on historical but not literary importance, or exclusively on Chinese art/philosophy.*

#### 9.3.1 Courses That Fulfill the Arts Requirement

Courses emphasizing integration of history, theory, aesthetics, and criticism. Performance/studio classes may count if their major emphasis is this integration (e.g., dance history, film art, history of architecture, history of modern art, history of/introduction to theatre, multicultural theatre, music history, jazz experience, music theory and analysis).

#### 9.3.2 Courses That Do Not Fulfill the Arts Requirement

Courses focused on technique, skills, or performance (e.g., beginning drawing, beginning painting, readers theater/oral interpretation focused on performance/skills-building) — see Section 5.4.1.

#### 9.3.3 Courses That Fulfill the Humanities Requirement

Courses encouraging analysis/appreciation of works of philosophical, historical, literary, aesthetic, religious, and cultural importance. Advanced foreign language/ESL courses (not principally skills-acquisition focused) may qualify if substantially literary/cultural. Theater and film courses may qualify if taught with emphasis on historical, literary, or cultural aspects.

#### 9.3.4 Courses That Do Not Fulfill the Humanities Requirement

English Composition, logic, speech, creative writing, oral interpretation, readers theatre, and all elementary language-other-than-English courses (skills/performance-focused) do not meet Area 3B specifications.

**9.3.5 Restriction on Unit Distribution:** A single course fulfills Area 3A; a separate course fulfills Area 3B. A course approved for both may satisfy only one.

### 9.4 Subject Area 4: Social and Behavioral Sciences

(2 courses: 6 semester or 8 quarter units; two academic disciplines required)

Fulfilled by two courses on how individuals, organizations, institutions, and societies interact/behave within socially-constructed dynamics. The two courses must be from two academic disciplines or an interdisciplinary sequence (e.g., an inherently interdisciplinary prefix like Social Justice Studies or Global Studies, or cross-listed courses like Psychology and Women's Studies). Coursework should develop understanding of social/behavioral science perspectives and research methods, examining problems in contemporary, historical, and geographical settings, and fostering appreciation of diverse genders, sexualities, races, ethnicities, classes, countries, cultures, and societies. Material should be theoretical, focused on core concepts/methods rather than personal, practical, or applied aspects.

*Note: CSU campuses may allow Area 4 courses to also satisfy the CSU U.S. History, Constitution and American Ideals (AI) graduation requirement. Certifying an Introduction to American Government course for Area 4 does not necessarily mean it satisfies the CSU American Institutions Graduation Requirement.*

#### 9.4.1 Courses That Do Not Fulfill the Social and Behavioral Sciences Requirement

Courses not taught from a social/behavioral science perspective (e.g., Physical Geography, Statistics) do not qualify and should be resubmitted in more appropriate areas. Courses with a practical, personal, career, or applied focus are not approved (Section 5.3.1). Courses like Administration of Justice may be approved if focused on core social/behavioral science concepts.

**9.4.2 Restriction on Unit Distribution:** Two courses in separate disciplines (Section 9.4) are required.

### 9.5 Subject Area 5: Physical and Biological Sciences

(At least 2 courses: 7 semester or 9 quarter units) — one course in each area required, and at least one must include a lab.

Fulfilled by one Area 5A (Physical Science) course and one Area 5B (Biological Science) course, at least one associated with a laboratory (Area 5C). Courses must emphasize experimental methodology, hypothesis testing, investigation, and systematic questioning/assessment rather than fact/data recall. Courses emphasizing interdependency of the sciences are especially appropriate for non-science majors.

#### 9.5.1 Courses That Fulfill Areas 5A and 5B

Courses focused on core concepts of a physical or biological science discipline (observation, hypothesis testing, evidence-based reasoning, fundamental theoretical principles and their application).

#### 9.5.2 Cal-GETC Laboratory Science Requirement (Area 5C)

Labs should rely on hands-on or validated-simulation manipulation of matter, equipment, and instrumentation, introducing safe equipment use. At least one of the two Area 5 courses must include a minimum of one unit of laboratory content, either as (i) a standalone lab course with an Area 5A/5B course as prerequisite/corequisite, or (ii) integrated into a combined lecture-and-lab course carrying Area 5A/5B approval. The lab and lecture must be related, since lab methodology builds on lecture principles. Lecture and lab may have separate course numbers. Lab courses must include a clearly identified lab manual in the COR.

#### 9.5.3 Restriction on Unit Distribution Including Unit Requirement for Laboratory Science Courses

3-semester/4-quarter-unit lab science courses may be used as long as the Area 5 minimum (7 semester/9 quarter units) is met. Stand-alone lab courses with a lecture prerequisite/corequisite must be a minimum of 1 semester/quarter unit.

- *Example 1:* 1 Biological Science w/lab (3 sem. units) + 1 Physical Science lecture (4 sem. units) → Area 5 satisfied.
- *Example 2:* 1 Biological Science w/lab (3 sem. units) + 1 Physical Science lecture (3 sem. units) + corresponding Physical Science lab (1 sem. unit) → Area 5 satisfied.

### 9.6 Ethnic Studies

(1 course: 3 semester or 4 quarter units)

CCC Area 6 courses may be written with both CSU and/or UC Ethnic Studies Core Competencies in mind, but must meet either the CSU or UC requirement — meeting one is deemed to meet the other. CCC Ethnic Studies courses predating AB 1460 (Weber, 2020) receive extra consideration during review.

#### 9.6.1 CSU's Definition of the Ethnic Studies Core Competencies Requirement

Fulfills CSU Education Code Section 89032; this 3-semester/4-quarter-unit requirement shall not be waived or substituted. Approved courses must have prefixes: African American, Asian American, Latina/o American, or Native American Studies (or similar, e.g., Pan-African Studies, American Indian Studies, Chicana/o Studies, Ethnic Studies); courses without such a prefix may qualify if cross-listed with one.

Courses must meet at least 3 of these 5 core competencies (campuses may add more):

1. Analyze and articulate concepts such as race and racism, racialization, ethnicity, equity, ethnocentrism, eurocentrism, white supremacy, self-determination, liberation, decolonization, sovereignty, imperialism, settler colonialism, and anti-racism, as studied in Native American, African American, Asian American, and/or Latina and Latino American Studies.
2. Apply theory/knowledge produced by these communities to describe critical events, histories, cultures, intellectual traditions, contributions, lived experiences, and social struggles, emphasizing agency and group-affirmation.
3. Critically analyze the intersection of race and racism with class, gender, sexuality, religion, spirituality, national origin, immigration status, ability, tribal citizenship, sovereignty, language, and/or age within these communities.
4. Critically review how struggle, resistance, racial and social justice, solidarity, and liberation are relevant to current structural issues (e.g., immigration, reparations, settler-colonialism, multiculturalism, language policies).
5. Describe and actively engage with anti-racist and anti-colonial issues, practices, and movements toward a just and equitable society.

CSU campuses may certify upper-division Ethnic Studies courses for this lower-division requirement if adequate lower-division options exist, and Ethnic Studies courses that are required elsewhere (major/minor/campus-wide) and approved for Area 6 also double-count for this requirement.

#### 9.6.2 UC's Definition of the Ethnic Studies Core Competencies Requirement

Approved courses must have prefixes reflecting the named Populations: African American, Asian American, Latina/o/x American, or Native American Studies (or similar, e.g., Black Studies, African Diaspora Studies, Pan African Studies, American Indian Studies, Indigenous Studies, Asian American & Asian Diaspora Studies, Asian American and Pacific Islander Studies, Chicana/o/x Studies, Latina/o/x Studies, Critical Race and Ethnic Studies); courses without such a prefix may qualify if cross-listed with one.

Courses must meet at least 3 of these 5 core competencies:

1. Analyze and articulate concepts such as race and racism, racialization, ethnicity, equity, ethnocentrism, eurocentrism, white supremacy, antiblackness, racial capitalism, self-determination, liberation, decolonization, sovereignty, imperialism, settler colonialism, exploitation colonialism, xenophobia, intersectionality, and anti-racism.
2. Apply theory/knowledge produced by the named Populations to understand critical events, histories, cultures, intellectual traditions, contributions, lived experiences, and social struggles, emphasizing subject formation, agency, and group affirmation.
3. Critically analyze the intersection of race and racism with class, gender, sexuality, religion, spirituality, national origin, immigration status, ability, tribal citizenship, sovereignty, language, and/or age within these communities.
4. Critically situate, in historical context, how struggle, resistance, racial and social justice, solidarity, and liberation are relevant to current structural issues at local, national, international, and transnational levels (e.g., immigration, reparations, settler colonialism, multiculturalism, language policies).
5. Describe and engage with anti-racist, abolitionist, and anti-colonial thought, issues, practices, and movements toward a more just and equitable society.

### 9.7 Requirements Outside of Cal-GETC

#### 9.7.1 U.S. History, Constitution, and American Ideals (AI) Requirement

The CSU AI graduation requirement is not part of Cal-GETC, though a course used for it may also be listed under a Cal-GETC subject area. CA Ed Code Title 5, Section 40404 (as last verified April 30, 2024) allows CCCs to certify completion of this CSU requirement. CSU campuses have discretion on whether courses not CCC-certified for AI may also count for GE.

#### 9.7.2 Language Other Than English (LOTE)

The UC LOTE graduation requirement is not part of Cal-GETC. Upon UC transfer, a course approved for a Cal-GETC subject area may satisfy LOTE if it meets relevant UC criteria.

## 10. Certification Processes

It is the student's responsibility to request Cal-GETC certification; each CCC has its own process. Completing Cal-GETC prior to transfer is strongly recommended for flexibility and timely progress to degree.

### 10.1 Who Certifies Cal-GETC?

Students who completed coursework at more than one CCC should have it certified by the last CCC attended for a regular term (fall/spring for semester schools; fall/winter/spring for quarter schools) prior to transfer. A CCC that is not the last school of attendance may certify at its discretion. Certification is processed without regard to current enrollment status or units accrued; the certification form is included with, or sent directly with, the student's transcript to the UC or CSU Office of Admissions.

### 10.2 Exceptions to the Certification Process

- **Cal-GETC certification using pre-Fall 2025 IGETC courses:** Students who completed IGETC-approved courses prior to Fall 2025 (including courses no longer active in assist.org) may apply them in the same Cal-GETC area(s).
- **IGETC certification using Cal-GETC courses:** Students seeking IGETC certification may use Cal-GETC-approved courses (new or newly approved Fall 2025+) in the same area(s).

These provisions accommodate exceptional cases where a student would otherwise repeat a similar GE course, expend more financial aid, exceed the transferable unit cap, or face other undue burdens.

### 10.3 Reviewing Coursework from Other Institutions

Coursework should be applied to the subject area in which it is listed by the institution where it was completed. *If College A certifies using coursework from College B, College A places it according to College B's approved list (see Section 5).*

#### 10.3.2 Coursework from All Other U.S. Institutions with ICAS-Approved Accreditation

Generally placed in the same subject areas as for the certifying community college (see Section 5.2 for details).

### 10.4 Instructions for Completing the Cal-GETC Certification Form

The certification form is completed by authorized CCC staff/faculty as determined by each college; the CCC Articulation Officer should have final review/determination and serve as the official liaison to the CSU and UC.

- For each Area, list course(s) taken, the college name, or the AP exam (minimum score of 3). AP cannot be used for Area 1B or 1C. List units (quarter or semester) in the "Units Completed" column.
- Full certification may be forwarded via a completed certification form, or by noting full Cal-GETC certification on the official (hard-copy or electronic) transcript.
- Courses must be passed with a minimum "C" grade ("C-" is not acceptable; "C" = 2.0/4.0). "Credit"/"Pass" is acceptable if equivalent to "C" or higher per institutional policy, reflected on transcript or catalog.
- The form must be signed and dated (no campus seal required) and come directly from the CCC to the UC/CSU campus(es) to be official; a copy is official if it has an official contact person, contact info, signature, or stamp.
- Students who attended more than one CCC should be certified by the last CCC attended for a regular term prior to transfer (per Section 10.1).
- See Section 7 for combining quarter/semester units within an Area; such conversions may require additional coursework to meet CSU's upper-division GE and graduation requirements.
- CCCs may also certify completion of the CSU AI graduation requirement and LOTE competence for Cal-GETC students, though neither is part of Cal-GETC itself.

#### 10.4.1 Certification of Competence in a Language Other Than English

UC-transferring students are encouraged (not required by Cal-GETC) to demonstrate LOTE competence equivalent to two years/second level of high school instruction, via:

- **Coursework:**
  - High school (English-language institution): second level of HS coursework in a LOTE, with "C-" or better in the second semester of the second year.
  - High school (non-English-language institution): formal schooling through sixth grade or higher, with documentation of language of instruction.
  - College courses: satisfactory completion (grade "C"/2.0 or "Credit/Pass" equivalent) of a course equivalent to second-level HS instruction — usually one semester of college LOTE work; equivalency is usually stated in the college catalog and reflected on the approved IGETC list / assist.org.
  - A Defense Language Institute Foreign Language Center (DLIFLC) LOTE course passed with "C" or higher.
- **Examinations:**
  - SAT Subject Tests in LOTE — minimum scores (pre-May 1995 / post-May 1995): Chinese w/ listening 500/520; French/French w/ listening 500/540; German/German w/ listening 500/510; Hebrew (Modern) 500/470; Italian 500/520; Japanese w/ listening 500/510; Korean/Korean w/ listening —/500; Latin 500/530; Spanish/Spanish w/ listening 500/520.
  - AP exams in LOTE — score of 3 or higher.
  - IB Higher Level exams in LOTE — score of 5 or higher.
  - GCE "O" Level exams in LOTE — grade A, B, or C. GCE "A" Level exams in LOTE — grade A, B, or C.
- **Other methods:**
  - Achievement test administered by a community college, university, or other college assessing proficiency equivalent to two years of HS LOTE, documented on transcript or official letterhead.
  - Faculty verification: if no achievement test is available, a faculty member at a U.S. regionally accredited institution may verify competency via a letterhead document.

## 13. Cal-GETC Standards Ongoing Governance

The Cal-GETC Standards are maintained by faculty of the UC, CSU, and CCC through their elected representatives on the Intersegmental Committee of Academic Senates (ICAS). The current roster and contact information for ICAS and its Cal-GETC advisory subcommittee are available at icas-ca.org.

Because of (i) the desired unity of Cal-GETC implementation across segments, (ii) the intersegmental nature of the standards, and (iii) the need for intersegmental alignment on implementation updates, further updates are adopted once authorized by ICAS and transmitted to the three segment offices (UC Office of the President, CSU Office of the Chancellor, and CCC Office of the Chancellor).
